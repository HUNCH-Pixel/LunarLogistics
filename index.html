NASA HUNCH · LLASO Project 1

Pixel Packers

We are the Pixel Packers! Our goal is to make a first-person simulation game that teaches players how to accurately manage, load, and unload a lunar container on the moon with minimal effort. [1]

Project

The idea

A replayable learning experience

Our game will consist of two phases: the Earth packing phase and the Moon unpacking phase. The player's goal is to pack/unpack as efficiently and as quickly as they can according to the mission they are given. [1]

The player will keep track of their inventory while ensuring stability within their stowage. We also plan to add a mission lander assistant (in-game) to help the player should they get stuck or have questions.

What we have settled on

Our container: 40 ft × 8 ft × 8 ft

We chose these dimensions for two main reasons. First, they best match the NASA HUNCH requirements. Second, they closely match real-life shipping containers, which gives us simpler calculations and more real-world data. [1]

Since we are planning around a 14-day mission, we have adjusted our inventory to best match a crew of four.

Our idea consists of moving the door to the middle of the container to cut down on how far a robot/human has to travel, and that a moving rack is worth a look. However, we are still looking into this!

Sizing a supply

A crew of four for 14 days

We multiply NASA's surface rates by 4 crew × 14 days = 56 crew-days. [2]

Container dimensions

Is the container too big?

NASA's early estimate for two crew on 14 days is about 37.5 single bags of 0.053 m³ each. [3][2] That is about 2 m³, or roughly 4 m³ for four crew (our own scaling).

Food and water alone will not fill 40 ft, so we have to explain what else belongs: racks, aisles, spares, tools and waste.

Box or tube?

Why we chose the box

40 × 8 × 8 ft is 2,560 ft³, about 72.5 m³ (× 0.0283). A 3 m by 12.19 m cylinder is about 86.2 m³, but a rectangle in a circle covers only 2/π ≈ 63.7%, leaving roughly 36% in the corners.

In our early run the box held 418 items to the cylinder's 325, and a middle door cut robot travel from about 20.2 to 10.1 ft.

However, the cylinder container is much easier to transport from Earth to the Moon due to its shape, so both models will be playable.

Borrowing ideas

NASA's packing contest

NASA ran a public contest for packing the Gateway logistics module. Winners favored simple, modular layouts with easy crew access. [4][5] That is the direction we keep coming back to: a design we can explain and a clear path through the container.
