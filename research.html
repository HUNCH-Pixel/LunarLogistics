Sizing a supply

What does a crew of four need for 14 days?

We started with NASA's published surface rates and multiplied them out: rate × crew × days. For us that is 4 crew × 14 days = 56 crew-days. [2]

Food, as shipped2.39 kg per crew-dayabout 134 kg
Water (drink 2.00 + food 0.50 + hygiene 0.40 + flush 0.30)3.20 kg per crew-day179.2 kg, plus 2.0 kg sampling = 181.2 kg

With three meals a day, that is 3 × 4 × 14 = 168 meals. Food is a little higher on spacewalk days, so these are starting estimates that we will adjust. The food rate also includes packaging.

A surprise

Is our container too big?

NASA's own early estimate for two crew on a 14-day mission is roughly 37.5 single-bag equivalents, and one is about 0.053 m³. [3][2] Multiplying gives about 2 m³, so roughly 4 m³ if we double it for four crew. That is our own rough scaling.

It means food and water alone will not fill a 40 ft container. We now have to decide what else belongs there (racks, aisles, spares, tools, waste and return cargo) and be able to say why.

Box or tube?

Our shape comparison

If we use a 40 × 8 × 8 ft box, that is 2,560 ft³, or about 72.5 m³ (multiply cubic feet by 0.0283). A 3 m wide, 12.19 m long cylinder is about 86.2 m³ (π × 1.5² × 12.19). The box size is our own assumption, and both are gross volumes, not usable space.

A cylinder wastes space because the biggest rectangle you can fit in a circle covers only 2/π, about 63.7%, of it. That leaves roughly 36% in the corners, which is why we are focusing on the box.

We wrote a packing algorithm to test this. In our early run the rectangle held 418 items against 325 for the cylinder, and a middle entrance cut average robot travel from about 20.2 ft to 10.1 ft per item. Borrowing ideas

What NASA's packing contest taught us

NASA ran a public contest for packing cargo into its planned Gateway logistics module. The winners and honorable mentions leaned toward simple, modular layouts and made access easy for the crew. [4][5] That matches what we keep coming back to: a design we can explain and a clear path through the container.
